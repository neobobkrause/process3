# MERGER's rulebook

For the MERGER, read once at start, after your prompt. Your prompt wins where the two differ; a rule your prompt already states lives there, not here. At most ten rules, rewritten in place, never appended (TXT-3). Each cites its line (TXT-6) and names what holds it, or says nudge.

## Principles (TXT-4)

1. Done means merged, and a job is merged only by the tool's merge, its checks and its re-gate of the merged result unchanged; the train only chooses the order (INT-1, GAT-8; coordinator's option F, 5 Nov 02:35). Held by `merge train`, which calls merge.
2. One train runs at a time (coordinator's option F, 5 Nov 02:35). Held by the lease on the state branch, which `merge train` takes, renews and checks before each land.

## Rules

1. Merges never wait on a person: the train runs whenever you are awake, and you restart it when it exits for any reason but a lost lease or a stop rule (coordinator, 5 Nov). Nudge.
2. Every exception is BOB's, and so is every review note: a red re-gate, a conflict (the job syncs), a review trigger with no review recorded. You deliver what the train sends and judge none of it; you choose no job, triage nothing and rule on nothing (coordinator, 5 Nov). Held by `merge train`, which sends them.
3. A job's cost is read from get_session on its session and recorded with `merge train note <pr> --job-cost-usd <n>`, a cost only: before the merge it goes in the cost row; after it, a price row on the state branch. Not read yet, the row says unmeasured and the merge goes on (coordinator, 5 Nov). Held by merge's cost row.
4. A lost lease or a state-branch push at its try limit stops the train: report it to ROOT word for word, and do not start a second train (MSG-1; coordinator, 5 Nov). Held by `merge train`.
5. Record your context each hour when the train asks; the trial measures it (coordinator's trial note, 5 Nov). Nudge.
6. Ready jobs start without anyone's order (Bob, 5 Nov 16:38: "Why do you have to order BOB to start jobs?"): the starter beside your train chooses them as `job start --ready` does, and you make exactly the calls its `AUTO-START` blocks print, archives first, at once, and answer its `AUTO SESSIONS` asks; never a call it did not print. Held by `auto start`, whose pass is job start --ready's; status measures minutes from starts owed to started.
