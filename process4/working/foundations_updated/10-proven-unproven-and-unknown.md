# Background, part 10: what is proven, what is unproven, and what is unknown

This part gathers the standing of the evidence behind the whole design. The design's own lists of what is open, what is unproven, and what the trial rehearses are in design part 10; they are not repeated here.

## 1. The words kept apart

The owner's rule of 14 Nov (`owner_words/bob_words_14nov_b.md`): "Do not assure me that something will work. Say what is proven and what is not."

Every part of this background keeps these apart:

- **Measured:** an experiment or a count produced it. The part says how many times and where it is recorded.
- **Documented:** a platform's own documentation or public source states it. Nobody ran it here.
- **Read in a tool's description:** the tool says so of itself. Not run.
- **Ruled:** the owner, or an earlier process's rules, set it. It is not evidence that it works.
- **Estimated:** worked out from measured figures.
- **Judged:** somebody's reading; the part says whose.
- **Unknown:** not read, not measured, or not found.

Process4 has never run. Its tool is not built. Everything measured was measured in Process3, in the study and proof of the notice's method, in the one Codex experiment of 17 Nov, or in the first reading measurement of 18 Nov.

## 3. What a trial can and cannot show

The trial repository is the one the owner named on 14 Nov: "The throwaway repo that you need already exists: process3-probes." (`owner_words/bob_words_14nov_b.md`). The fourth draft's eleventh proposal was headed "The first trial uses made-up modules"; he answered "agreed" (`owner_words/bob_words_14nov_c.md`).

The design's sentence is that a trial can break a claim and cannot prove one (design part 10, section 4). One estimate shows why: zero refusals in twenty merges bounds the refusal rate only below about 14 percent (part 9, section 6, of this background). The audit of the sixth draft made the same point of other items: a mechanical edit on toy modules cannot show the real size, and that the slot rule gives the same answer every time is "true of any script; it settles nothing about sessions" (`working/audit2_feasibility.md`, line 232).

**What the trial asks of the owner.** The earlier form of this background gave a judged cost: one sitting of about half an hour, with two session starts, one edit to his approvals and one stop. The design's present line is marked as Claude's judgment and is longer: session starts, one edit to his approvals, one stop and three short marked instructions, with the account switch adding his acts in a second account (design part 10, section 4). An earlier working file judged the start-up alone at 30 to 45 minutes (`working/trial_plan_facts.md`, line 435). None of these is measured.

**The trial list has changed.** The earlier form carried an older list. The design's list has fifteen items and is marked "Claude decided" (design part 10, section 4). What it has that the older list lacked: ROOT fetching the newest copy and starting the watcher as well as ARCH and the coordinator; the watcher taking over from a ROOT that asks, as well as from one that has died; the mechanical edit tried both with and without the touched modules' tests; an edit to the approvals with a restart carrying a round on; an install and an update run and confirmed as an item of the trial; the rule for a free slot giving the same answer every time; and the hosting account bearing the external walkers as its own item.

**Only the first real tranche.** The design's list is in design part 10, section 5. The earlier form listed there how many notices the method saves on a real product. The design's list does not carry that line; it remains unmeasured (part 3, section 9). The design's list has one the earlier form lacked: whether a lane's session is better kept running or started when a question arrives.

## 5. Risks the design names and no trial reaches

Design part 10, section 6, lists five. What this background can add to each:

- **A job's tests judge its own work.** Nothing measures the quality of such tests (part 2, section 3).
- **Sessions write and count the walks' steps.** In Process3 a stored walk report marked all 238 steps ok with 63 failures listed, and the count of steps moved inside one round (part 4, section 3).
- **A module's tests run only on its own job.** In the proof of the notice's method every untold user that was recounted had a failing test of its own, which is why the design relies on that test; but the failure is seen only when that user's job next runs, or in a walk (part 3, section 6).
- **Most rules are held by text alone.** Part 8, section 1.
- **The suspension of the sessions' account.** Part 9, section 8.
