# Background, part 7: how the owner's words become decisions

This part gives the reasons and the evidence behind design part 1, sections 4 and 5, and design part 4, section 9. It states no rule. The owner is Bob; the files of his typed words carry his name.

## 1. Why this needed a rule

The owner's working rule of 14 Nov (`owner_words/bob_words_14nov_b.md`): "Only words I wrote count as my word. Never record your decision or your summary as mine."

The record shows the thing he was guarding against, more than once.

- **Process3.** An audit of its record found that of 21 items marked as his, 14 were mechanisms a session had chosen, and that 7 of 35 safeguards quoted him (`working/audit2_corruption.md`, line 390, from `evidence/causal_forensics.md`, section 4.1).
- **The drafts of Process4.** The fourth draft set apart a group of lines with the warning: "These reached this document through a session's written record, not through text you typed." (`definition/process4_definition_v4.md`, line 46).
- **The seventh draft.** A check of every passage in quotation marks found five that are in no file of his typed words and reach the design only through the sixth draft, and one found only in the design state (`working/readiness_18nov/draft7_quotations_checked.md`). The design now says of each which record it comes through. This background does the same: part 2, section 4 (the record of 13 Nov); part 5, section 3 (his ledger note); part 8, section 3 (the field report); part 4, section 4 (the earlier process's decision record); part 6, section 6 (the design state).
- **Codex.** The Codex work's own register says he approved a layout with ROOT as a child of the chat he opened. His five files of Codex words do not hold those words (`working/codex_shortcomings_to_fill.md`, under "Not checkable from this repository").

How often a session turns conversation into a decision has not been counted.

No file of his typed words holds them. The check of quotations confirms that the marks and the question are the design's own terms and not quotations of his.

**His latest statement replaces an earlier one.** From the same file, section 0. The record gives three cases where applying it changed the design:

- How a tranche ends: the record of 13 Nov, then his typed words of 14 Nov (part 4, section 1, of this background).
- Whether a window of a million tokens is required: his words of 17 Nov in one file, replaced by his words of 17 Nov in another (part 6, section 4). Five working files of the Codex work still carried the older requirement when a worker compared them (`working/harness_separation/03_codex_maximums_figures.md`, section 7).
- The fill level: five values over three weeks (part 6, section 6).

(`owner_words/bob_words_14nov_b.md`).

## 3. Two records, and the front door

**Two records.** That a product's decisions go through the coordinator and a change to the process through PROCESS, each with its own record, is from the review session of 18 Nov (`working/design_state_18nov.md`, section 4). The separation of PROCESS is his typed word of 14 Nov (part 8 of this background).

**The front door.** To the Codex session of 16 and 17 Nov (`owner_words/bob_words_16_17nov_codex_development.md`, an assistant's selection of extracts):

> We need to address the issue of my being able to interact with the sessions. Yes, the coordinator is the front door. But interacting with lanes is crucial, as this is where architecture and design decisions are made. I rarely interact with jobs, but seeing their progress and status is important. ARCH used to be the front door, and it continues to play a very important role. I of course need to interact with PROCESS when issues arise.

And: "Just to name it - all of these sessions may need to stay active beyond the completion of their initial scope of work. So these are truly and fully interactive sessions."

**Changing the slot number.** The earlier form of this background said, here and in part 3, that he changes it by telling the coordinator the new value. The design says he changes it by a marked instruction (design part 2, section 5).

## 4. How a question is put to him

The one form for a question is from the review session of 18 Nov (`working/design_state_18nov.md`, section 4). ## 5. What he can see of the work

His words are in section 3: he rarely speaks to jobs, "but seeing their progress and status is important".

**Grouping.** On 17 Nov, of Codex (`owner_words/bob_words_17nov_project_membership.md`): "Again, I see no need for subagents to be members of a project. Only jobs, lanes, ARCH, ROOT, and the process coordinator sessions should be kept in the project."

**Archiving.** To the Codex session: "Under Claude, sessions that are no longer active are archived so that they don't clog up the session list. There's no downside to archiving sessions because they can be found, reviewed, and even unarchived."

**What carries these.** On Claude Code the design marks its choices as "Claude decided": a tag naming the product, and a title naming the module. Both are read in the session tools' own descriptions and neither has been measured (design part 7, section 1). On Codex the earlier form of this background said that how sessions are grouped and archived is not yet known. Archiving was since found to be not available to a hosted session, measured as absent. Grouping is still not known (design part 8, section 1).

## 6. The standing page and the notification

Both are from the review session of 18 Nov (`working/design_state_18nov.md`, sections 4 and 14). The design marks as "Claude decided" who keeps the page and who sends the notification (design part 1, section 5).

One measured fact shows what a notification is worth when it is not tied to something only he can clear: after Process3 was stopped, its hourly task sent him 94 push notifications in four days (part 5, section 10, of this background).

Why a page as well as a notification: his own paragraph on PROCESS says "I might be away from my computer for extended periods" (`owner_words/bob_words_14nov_b.md`).

On Codex what carries a notification, and what carries the page, are not known (design part 8, section 1).

## 7. Renewing a token, and the Codex sign-in

**Tokens in general.** That every reasonable effort is made to renew a token before it expires, so that no full stop is needed, is from the review session of 18 Nov (`working/design_state_18nov.md`, section 14).

**The Codex sign-in.** The design says the owner expects Codex to need its sign-in renewed and the process to support that cleanly (design part 8, section 2). His words, to the Codex session of 16 and 17 Nov:

> Yes, Codex process4 should be prepared to handle if OpenAI invalidates the renewable authorization, I revoke it, or all valid refresh credentials are lost. That process may require my involvement - as each of these situations sound to me like account level conditions that I need to be involved in correcting. But these must be exceptional conditions that don't occur as part of my Codex account UX.

> It might be reasonable to ask me to be involved in refreshing the token before the 30 day expiration date. But the Codex version of process4 must recognize and cleanly support that act.

How it would be done is not known. One Codex trial found the command-line client "Logged in using ChatGPT" while its request to list tasks was refused with "401 Unauthorized" (`working/harness_separation/01_neutral_terms.md`, section 3). Nothing more is recorded.

## 8. What the subscription covers

The design brings anything that would cost more than the subscription to him first (design part 1, section 3). His words, to the Codex session, when an assistant proposed a separately billed machine: "Is this a dealbreaker? It was my understanding that the VM would be one running in OpenAI's infrastructure and the cost of which would be covered as part of my ChatGPT subscription. (That's how Claude Code Remote works.) Are you in fact designing something that will require additional costs outside of the subscription?" The file notes that the proposal was then rejected.

Where cost can be read on Claude Code is open, for a session to find (design part 7, section 1, last row). In the first reading measurement a separately started command-line session ran on another model by default, "and how it is billed could not be read" (`working/reading_measurements/RESULTS.md`, section 7).
